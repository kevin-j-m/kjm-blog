## Rocky Mountain Ruby 2026

This post is not intended as a review of any of the talks, but to highlight the variety of great work from all involved. I hope you’ll seek out the full videos of all the sessions that interest you once they are available.

## Day 1

### InstiLLMent of Successful Practices in an Agentic World

Thanks to everyone for joining me to start the program.

{{< figure src="/img/rmr_2026_kevin.jpg" class="mid" alt="Kevin at Rocky Mountain Ruby 2026" >}}
📸 Credit: Rachael Wright-Munn

### Confident On-Call: Building Incident Response Skills as a Ruby Engineer

Sonja Peterson provided an excellent case to demonstrate how on-call work can be improved through better rotation onboarding, active incident management, and intentional post-mortems.

{{< figure src="/img/rmr_2026_sonya.jpg" class="mid" alt="Sonya at Rocky Mountain Ruby 2026" >}}

### Beyond Senior: Consider the staff path!

Joel Hawksley shared how Staff Engineer experiences differ widely, starting by sharing his progression and expanding to what he's learned through interviews with other Staff colleagues.

{{< figure src="/img/rmr_2026_joel.jpg" class="mid" alt="Joel at Rocky Mountain Ruby 2026" >}}

### Modeling Class Hierarchies in Active Record

Nicolas Erlichman provided the benefits and downsides to single table inheritance (STI), pure abstract classes, and delegated types.

{{< figure src="/img/rmr_2026_nicholas.jpg" class="mid" alt="Nicholas at Rocky Mountain Ruby 2026" >}}

### Slowly We Rot: signs of a Rails app’s decay

Fernando Perales demonstrated how flakey tests, unoptimized factory usage, the wrong level of testing, and code pattern deviations can lead to rot in your application.

{{< figure src="/img/rmr_2026_fernando.jpg" class="mid" alt="Fernando at Rocky Mountain Ruby 2026" >}}

### Commit for Good

Ben Reubenstein presented a case for engaging in skills-based volunteering. Beyond the obvious benefits to the organization, you get to try new things and meet new people.

{{< figure src="/img/rmr_2026_ben.jpg" class="mid" alt="Ben at Rocky Mountain Ruby 2026" >}}

### Your own AI Agent in ~400 lines of Ruby

Drew Bragg utilized HTTP, JSON, and loops to build an AI agent - it's just that simple!

{{< figure src="/img/rmr_2026_drew.jpg" class="mid" alt="Drew at Rocky Mountain Ruby 2026" >}}

### Got AI ontological shock? No worries: UFOs, Theology, and Psychedelics with Ruby-Colored Reverence

Travis Turner compared AI's impact on humans with other experiences like aliens, drugs, and religion.

{{< figure src="/img/rmr_2026_travis.jpg" class="mid" alt="Travis at Rocky Mountain Ruby 2026" >}}

## Day 2

### Herb in Rails 8.2: Your ERB views, now HTML-aware

Marco Roth presented two talks in his session (by accident). I was impressed by all the LSP features in Herb. I'm intrigued by the experimental `Async` component, but am curious how that may discourage separation of concerns.

{{< figure src="/img/rmr_2026_marco.jpg" class="mid" alt="Marco at Rocky Mountain Ruby 2026" >}}

### High-Octane Onboarding: Ramping into a Rails Codebase with AI Agents

Andrea Fomera shared best practices for an onboarding experience guided by AI.

{{< figure src="/img/rmr_2026_andrea.jpg" class="mid" alt="Andrea at Rocky Mountain Ruby 2026" >}}

### Optimize Your Mindset (Without Overclocking)

Christine Seeman presented a series of strategies for applying mindfulness, habit building, growth mindset, and deep work into your life.

{{< figure src="/img/rmr_2026_christine.jpg" class="mid" alt="Christine at Rocky Mountain Ruby 2026" >}}

### Building an Agentic RAG in the Rails Way with RubyLLM

Giovanni Panasiti presented how to utilize RubyLLM in Rails apps.

{{< figure src="/img/rmr_2026_giovanni.jpg" class="mid" alt="Giovanni at Rocky Mountain Ruby 2026" >}}

### Fewer Tests, More Confidence: A Rails Testing Philosophy for the AI Era

Ifat Ribon demonstrated how to test behavior (not implementation), keep tests deterministic, balance coverage with cost, and use good test design to promote good code design.

{{< figure src="/img/rmr_2026_ifat.jpg" class="mid" alt="Ifat at Rocky Mountain Ruby 2026" >}}

This was my first time at Rocky Mountain Ruby. It was a comfortable space that provided an opportunity for deep connection. Thanks to the organizers and volunteers for coming together so that __we__ could all come together during the event.
